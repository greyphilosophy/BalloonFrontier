# Hydrogen vs Helium: Which Gas Should You Choose?

This tutorial compares hydrogen and helium for weather balloon flights—focusing on lift, cost/availability, and real-world safety trade-offs.

---

## TL;DR (player decision)
- Choose **helium** if you want **more safety margin** and a smoother “beginner-friendly” experience.
- Choose **hydrogen** if you need **the highest lift** and you can tolerate **higher flammability/leak risk**.

Hydrogen gives only **~8–9% more buoyant lift** than helium for the same envelope volume—but that small advantage comes with much bigger safety and containment considerations.

---

## 1) Lift characteristics (the key physics)
Buoyant lift per unit volume is approximately:

- lift ≈ density(air) − density(gas)

Using typical near-sea-level conditions (rough, but useful):
- density(air) ≈ **1.225 kg/m³**
- density(helium) ≈ **0.1785 kg/m³**
- density(hydrogen) ≈ **0.0899 kg/m³**

Estimated lift (kg per m³ of gas):

| Gas | Lift ≈ ρ_air − ρ_gas | Relative to helium |
|---|---:|---:|
| Helium | 1.225 − 0.1785 ≈ **1.047 kg/m³** | **1.00×** |
| Hydrogen | 1.225 − 0.0899 ≈ **1.135 kg/m³** | **~1.085×** (≈ +8.5%) |

What that means in practice:
- Same envelope volume → **hydrogen carries ~8.5% more payload** (before you account for envelope mass, ballast, valves, etc.).
- Same payload goal → hydrogen can often use a **slightly smaller volume** for the same raw buoyancy.

Important: lift is only one piece. Envelope mass, burst limits, leak rate, and fill/pressure strategy all affect your real outcome.

---

## 2) Cost & availability (what your “budget” buys)
**Helium**
- Generally **more expensive** and **less globally abundant**.
- Often supplied in regulated cylinders/containers.

**Hydrogen**
- Often **cheaper** (or at least easier to source in bulk), depending on your region.
- But you still need a safe, regulated way to obtain and handle it.

In a game sense: treat helium as the “pay more, worry less” option, and hydrogen as the “optimize performance, accept higher risk” option.

---

## 3) Safety trade-offs (why hydrogen isn’t just “better”)
### Hydrogen: high energy + fast leaks
Hydrogen is:
- **Highly flammable** (small ignition sources can matter)
- **Low-molecular-weight** → it can **diffuse and leak through materials faster**

So the practical dangers aren’t only “it burns”—it’s also that you may get a **concentration in unintended places** if containment isn’t excellent.

### Helium: inert, lower hazard
Helium is:
- **Inert** (doesn’t readily burn)
- Still a leak concern (nothing is perfectly sealed), but it’s **far easier to treat as a low-ignition-risk gas**.

---

## 4) Leak & containment (envelope lifetime matters)
Because hydrogen molecules are much smaller, hydrogen tends to:
- **escape faster**
- reduce effective flight time sooner unless your envelope and fittings are designed for it

Helium also leaks, but typically **slower**, which often means:
- a more predictable flight window
- fewer “lost buoyancy” surprises mid-mission

---

## 5) So when should you pick which?
### Pick helium when:
- You’re starting out or want the **highest safety margin**.
- Your mission values **predictable flight time** over maximum payload.
- Your setup has less tolerance for “risk multipliers” (valves, materials, or environment safety tiers).

### Pick hydrogen when:
- You need **maximum lift** and are willing to accept higher risk.
- You have the right “safety tier” equipment/mechanics available in your game.

---

## 6) Common misconceptions
- “Hydrogen floats *way* higher.”
  - In terms of raw buoyant lift, it’s only **~8–9% more** than helium.
- “Helium is perfectly safe.”
  - Helium is far safer than hydrogen, but it can still leak; treat all gases with care.
- “Hydrogen is fine as long as there’s no flame.”
  - Real risk is broader than visible flames: ignition sources can be easy to miss.

---

## Quick checklist before launch (gas choice)
1. Do you prioritize **safety margin** (helium) or **max lift** (hydrogen)?
2. Is your envelope/systems design consistent with the **expected leak behavior**?
3. Are you accounting for real payload needs beyond raw buoyancy (valves, envelope mass, ballast)?
4. Does your mission setting penalize high-risk gas choices?
5. If hydrogen: ensure your game setup includes the appropriate **containment/safety tier**.
6. Re-check burst constraints (more lift can still lead to failure if burst limits are exceeded).

---

## One-sentence summary
Hydrogen is the higher-lift gas by about **~8.5%**, but helium is the safer, more predictable choice—so pick hydrogen only when your risk controls (and mission goals) justify it.
